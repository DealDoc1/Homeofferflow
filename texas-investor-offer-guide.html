Organize an offer in a practical order.

1. Confirm the intended buyer.
Keep entity authority, contacts, and any details needing professional confirmation together. Do not reuse an old entity assumption because it was right for a prior acquisition.
2. Separate property facts from assumptions.
Organize source documents, access questions, due-diligence items, and facts that still require verification before a decision is made.
3. Consider complete terms, not only price.
Financing context, timing, earnest money, option, possession, contingencies, concessions, and other conditions can materially affect what you are proposing.
4. Set review checkpoints early.
List questions for the appropriate attorney, broker, lender, title company, inspector, tax professional, or other qualified adviser before the deadline creates pressure.
5. Reuse defaults carefully.
Use saved investor defaults for recurring operational details, but independently review every new offer and never carry forward property-specific facts without confirmation.

Investor offer readiness checklist.

  • The intended purchaser/entity and authority questions to confirm.
  • Property facts, due-diligence items, and source documents still to verify.
  • Funding, timing, and contingency questions for the appropriate adviser.
  • Complete terms to evaluate together—not only price.
  • Review questions and the person responsible for each answer.
  • A plan to review a supported packet before any payment or signature step.
