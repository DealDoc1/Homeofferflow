Texas FSBO planning guide

Plan your Texas FSBO sale before you list, negotiate, or accept an offer.

Selling without a traditional listing is less about doing every job yourself and more about knowing which decision comes next. This guide helps you organize the work, identify when to get appropriate professional support, and keep a clear record of your plan.

Start with your goal and timeline.

A useful plan begins with the decision you are trying to make: prepare the property, test a sale-by-owner path, seek MLS exposure, compare incoming offers, or organize closing steps. Write down your ideal timing, any hard deadline, the property’s current condition, and which parts you want help with. That gives you a practical way to compare support options without committing before you are ready.

Work through the sale in a sensible order.

1
Prepare the property.
Make a short, prioritized list of repairs, cleanup, photos, access, and showing-readiness tasks. Separate must-do safety or condition work from improvements you are only considering.
2
Choose your exposure plan.
Decide how prospective buyers will find the property and how you will manage inquiries. If you are considering MLS exposure or any licensed service, confirm the provider, scope, availability, and terms directly before relying on it.
3
Keep showing information organized.
Use one place for property facts, showing questions, and follow-up. Avoid sharing sensitive information unnecessarily, and pause when a question requires a qualified professional rather than a planning answer.
4
Compare offers as complete packages.
Price is one part of an offer. Organize financing, timelines, contingencies, concessions, possession, and closing questions together so the appropriate reviewer can evaluate the whole picture.
5
Confirm the right closing support.
Before you sign or proceed, identify which title, legal, brokerage, lending, inspection, tax, insurance, or other support is appropriate for your particular transaction.

A practical FSBO readiness checklist.

  • Property address and a realistic target timeline.
  • Photos, repair, cleaning, or showing-prep priorities.
  • A plan for buyer inquiries and showing access.
  • A way to keep offer and follow-up information together.
  • Questions for the qualified professionals you may need before committing.
  • A clear understanding that any paid or regulated service has separate scope, provider, and pricing confirmation.

Get help without giving up control.

Many sellers need help with only a portion of the path. You might begin with property-prep planning, consider a limited-service MLS option, organize offer-review questions, or build a contract-to-close readiness list. The useful question is not whether you must choose one all-in path; it is what you need next and who is responsible for it.

No. A seller request does not create a brokerage relationship, legal representation, transaction, or provider selection. Any regulated or paid service is confirmed separately with the appropriate provider.

What should I organize before accepting an offer?

Keep price, financing, timeline, contingencies, concessions, and closing questions together. Use the appropriate licensed, title, legal, tax, lending, inspection, or other professional support for decisions outside a planning workflow.
