Begin with the decision you are making.

Identify the property, what you have actually confirmed about it, and the decision you need to make next. You may be deciding whether to request more information, confirm financing, prepare an offer for review, or pause for a qualified professional's input. Keeping those decisions separate makes the process less confusing.

Organize the offer in a practical order.

1
Confirm the property facts you are relying on.
Keep the address, listing information, timing questions, and items you need clarified together. Do not fill gaps with assumptions when an answer should come from an appropriate source.
2
Know your financing context.
Organize the information you have from your lender or financing source and note what needs confirmation. Financing decisions should be handled with the appropriate lender or adviser.
3
Think in complete terms, not just price.
Timing, financing, option, earnest money, possession, concessions, property questions, and other conditions can all affect what you are proposing.
4
List questions before time pressure.
Write down questions for your agent, attorney, lender, title company, inspector, insurance adviser, or other qualified professional before you need an immediate answer.
5
Review before payment or signature.
Pause to correct information and use the appropriate professional review for decisions outside a form-completion workflow.

A buyer readiness checklist.

  • The property address and facts you still need to confirm.
  • Your proposed timing and any hard deadlines.
  • Financing context and questions for your lender or adviser.
  • The offer terms you want to consider together, not only price.
  • Questions for qualified professionals before commitment.
  • A plan to review the completed offer summary before checkout or signing.
